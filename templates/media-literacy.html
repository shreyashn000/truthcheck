DIGITAL MEDIA LITERACY

Think Before
You Share.

Not everything you see online is true. Learn simple ways to identify misleading information, verify claims and make better decisions before sharing.

Learn How To Verify
WHY IT MATTERS

Information spreads fast. Verification takes time.

Social media and messaging platforms make it easy to receive and share information within seconds. However, false or misleading information can spread just as quickly.

Digital media literacy means developing the habit of questioning information, checking its source and looking for reliable evidence before accepting or sharing it.

01 — SPOT THE WARNING SIGNS

Does the information
look suspicious?

Look for these common warning signs before trusting or forwarding a piece of information.

01

Sensational Headline

Extremely shocking, emotional or exaggerated headlines may be designed to attract attention.

02

Unknown Source

Be careful when the information does not identify a trustworthy publisher or original source.

03

No Date or Author

Missing publication dates or authors can make information difficult to verify.

04

Pressure to Share

Messages saying “share immediately” or “forward to everyone” should be treated cautiously.

05

Suspicious Links

Check website addresses carefully. Look for unusual domains, spelling mistakes or misleading URLs.

06

Manipulated Media

Images, videos and audio can be edited or generated. Visual evidence should also be verified.

02 — VERIFY BEFORE SHARING

Use the 5-step check.

When you receive a suspicious claim, follow these steps instead of immediately forwarding it.

1

Check the source

Find out who originally published the information.

2

Check the date

Old news can be reshared as if it were recent.

3

Look for evidence

Check whether the claim provides credible evidence or supporting information.

4

Compare sources

Check important claims against multiple reliable sources.

5

Pause before sharing

If you cannot verify it, do not forward it as established fact.

VERIFICATION
EXAMPLE CLAIM

“Government announces a new benefit. Share this with everyone immediately!”

WHAT SHOULD YOU DO?

Do not share immediately. Search for the announcement on an official government website or another reliable source.
